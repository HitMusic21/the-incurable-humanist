### 1. Teresa Margolles at MoMA PS1 ⭐ TOP PICK

**What:** First US survey. **Opens September 24, 2026**, MoMA PS1, through
Feb 2027. Plus a companion installation at MoMA Manhattan through spring 2027.
Curated by Ana Janevski and Inés Katzenstein (Patricia Phelps de Cisneros
Research Institute).

**The hook:** Margolles trained as a **forensic pathologist**. Her subject is
violence at the US–Mexico border — disappearances, femicides, displacement.
Centerpiece *La promesa* (2012) is **22 tons of rubble** from a house abandoned
in Ciudad Juárez. The MoMA piece drips water infused with residue from sites
where people were murdered onto heated steel plates, hissing into steam.

**Why it's the top pick:**
- **Her exact credential.** A Christie's-trained art historian writing on a
  major museum survey is unimpeachable authority — not an opinion, an expertise.
- **Grief + migration + the material trace of the dead** — her three themes in
  one artist, without Venezuela.
- **No reviews published yet.** She can be early rather than late.
- Latin American, so her fluency transfers, but Mexican — widening the lane.

**Angle:** What does it mean to memorialise with the physical residue of the
dead? Margolles refuses the clean monument. Denise has written repeatedly about
what grief leaves behind materially.

**Timing: publish September 25–30.** This is the highest-value window in the doc.

### 2. Grieving a chatbot — "patch-breakups" ⭐ HIGHEST TRAFFIC CEILING

**What:** When OpenAI removed GPT-4o, users grieved like the bereaved.
***Nature Human Behaviour* (2026), "Mourning the loss of AI companions"**:
**54,861 Reddit posts**, 1,452 participants across seven surveys. Company
updates trigger **separation distress**. Researchers coined **"patch-breakup."**
A systematic review frames it as **disenfranchised grief** — a loss no one will
license you to mourn.

**Why it converts:** AI + grief is the largest addressable intersection
available to her. Enormous existing search and social demand, and almost
nobody writing it with genuine grief literacy. Most AI coverage is technical or
alarmist; hers would be neither.

**Her angle:** She is one of the few writers who can say *this is what grief
actually does* and be believed. **Disenfranchised grief** is the concept that
makes the essay — and it connects to her existing readers, who know that
feeling from migration.

**Pairs with:** **Griefbots.** A CU Boulder study (ACM DIS, June 2026) had 16
grieving volunteers interact with AI versions of their dead. **Sherry Turkle**
supplies the thesis: holding a simulation robs the bereaved of the
metabolisation that turns the dead into an **internal, sustaining presence**.
One woman cloned her father's voice and now feels he is "removed outside of me
and lives inside of a computer" — the bot also hallucinated a camping trip that
never happened.

### 3. "I would die if I were you"

**What:** Emily Rapp Black's *I Would Die If I Were You* (Counterpoint, May
2026). The title is the worst thing anyone said to her about her loss. Her
comment: *"It's so rude."* Meant to convey strength, it creates distance.

**Why:** The smallest, sharpest, most shareable idea in the research — **the
failure mode of sympathetic language**. Everyone has been on one side of this.
High Notes potential; the title alone is a Note.

**Her angle:** She has been on the receiving end of condolence language in two
registers — personal loss and the things people say about a collapsing
country. The essay writes itself, and it is *entirely* outside the Venezuela lane
while drawing on the same well.

### 4. Edwidge Danticat, *Dèy* — the book match

**What:** Knopf, Aug 25, 2026. First novel in a decade. **The title means
"mourning" in Haitian Kreyòl.** Florida, 2018: Magnolia, a Haitian American
realtor, survives a mall shooting and tells no one; her parents in Haiti are
threatened by gangs; her mother shows signs of dementia. Structured by
shelter-in-place protocol: "Run," "Hide," "F(l)ight," "Dèy."
**"Rave" across 7 reviews. National Book Award Fiction longlist** — finalists
**Oct 6**.

**Why:** Grief, migration, diaspora, violence, dementia — nearly all her themes
in one book, by a major Caribbean diaspora writer. Danticat's readership is
adjacent to hers and much larger.

**Her angle:** A response essay, not a review — the form that worked for her
Elizabeth Gilbert piece (183 views).

**Timing: before October 6** to ride the NBA finalist news.

### 5. The immigration opinion gap ⭐ MOST QUOTABLE STATISTIC

**What:** **Gallup (June 2026):** nearly **three in four** Americans say
immigration is good; **only 29%** want it reduced. Among Republicans, desire to
curb fell **40 points** (88% → 48%). **Pew (April 2026): 52%** say the
administration deports too much.

Meanwhile: **65,765** in ICE detention (record), ~**1,600 arrests/day**,
**356,389** removals in FY2026.

**The line:** *a democracy accelerating against its own electorate.*

**Why:** Her immigration-consultant credential makes this authoritative rather
than opinionated — and it is immigration writing with **no Venezuela in it.**

**Handle with care:** keep it structural, not partisan. Her form is reflection,
not news commentary.

## Tier 2 — Strong, more specific

### 6. "Artists are leaving the city for the first time in a really long time"

NYC approved a **record $323.8M** cultural budget for FY2027 — highest ever,
**$100M+ above** the City Council's own plan — plus a first-ever **Cultural
Stability Fund** ($10M/year through FY2029) covering individual artists, created
after Annie Dorsen and Lynn Nottage testified about federal rescissions.

The quoted line belongs to new Cultural Affairs Commissioner **Diya Vij**.
**The paradox: unprecedented public investment arriving exactly as
practitioners leave.** Badly under-covered outside the art press. Her NYC art
world + immigration lens is unusually well suited.

### 7. Ambient enforcement fear in immigrant New York

**Dec 4, 2025:** Jackson Heights woke to helicopters and masked federal officers
with a warrant naming **no particular resident**. **811 "collateral" arrests in
NYC since August; 85% had no criminal history.** A Queens EDC survey of 66
businesses found **nearly 80% reported decreased sales**. New international
arrivals to NYC fell **~70%**, and **Washington Heights, Chinatown, Astoria,
Sunset Park and East Flatbush all lost foreign-born population.**

One neighbour: *"Everybody just pretty much stays within their fence now. Like
nobody wants to cross the threshold anymore."*

**The mechanism is ambient, not episodic** — fear as a condition rather than an
event. That distinction is the essay, and it is a register she already writes in.

### 8. The Armory Show's Caribbean theme

**Sept 24–27, Javits.** Both curated sections take **the Caribbean and its
diasporas** as their premise; **El Museo del Barrio** holds the Spotlight booth.
- **Lucia Hierro, *Nuestra Silla*** — the plastic chair of Dominican
  neighbourhoods as monumental sculpture
- **Bony Ramirez, *La Carta*** — on receiving devastating news

**Diasporic domestic objects carrying grief** is a subject she could own. Very
short window; pairs naturally with Margolles as a single "diaspora art week"
piece.

### 9. The death of *Book World* — criticism's collapse

**Feb 4, 2026:** the Washington Post eliminated its books section. Two decades
ago ~30–50 newspapers ran standalone book sections; **now the NYT alone.**
**Fewer than ten full-time book critics work in the US** — the circulating line
is that there are twice as many humans in space.

Connects to the **Lauren Groff / Substack fight**: Groff called Substack
*"anti-community, anti-new-writer"*; Lincoln Michel and John Warner called that
"exactly backwards." Denise is a working example of the thing being argued
about. ⚠️ The Groff quote may be out of context — attribute carefully.

### 10. Ana Mendieta at Tate Modern

**Through Jan 17, 2027.** Biggest-ever UK show of the Cuban exile artist. The
live critical argument is **shifting focus off her biography and Carl Andre's
trial back onto the work** — a debate about how we let a woman artist's death
consume her art. Exile, body, earth, disappearance. Already reviewed, so
there's a conversation to enter rather than start.

## Distribution — more important than topic choice

From `ICP_AND_GROWTH_ANALYSIS.md`: **Notes produced 58 subscribers; Google
produced 4.** No topic choice overcomes that ratio.

For each essay:
1. **Extract 2–3 Notes.** The sharpest line, standing alone. "I would die if I
   were you" is already a Note.
2. **Post the Note before the essay.** Notes that teach something outperform
   promotional links.
3. **Instagram** is her best non-Substack channel (15 subscribers from 411
   visits). Art-world essays with images suit it.

⚠️ Most published Substack-growth advice comes from writers selling growth
courses. Treat specific subscriber-count claims sceptically; the one number here
that isn't sceptical is her own: 58 vs 4.

## One honest caveat on "viral"

The brief asked for topics that will "make us go viral." Nothing here can
promise that, and any list that claimed to would be selling something.

What this list does offer: topics with **demonstrably large existing audiences**
where Denise has **genuine authority** — which is the reproducible version of
the thing virality gestures at. Her publication grew 14× in 17 months on
consistency, not on a viral hit. The single largest day in her history was
**+10 subscribers.**

The realistic goal is **reaching a larger audience per essay** and converting at
her normal rate — not a spike. The Margolles piece is the best shot at the
spike, because she'd be first.
